Advisor Research Desk

1

Before a client conversation, an advisor has to check a stock's price by hand and then read long SEC filings. Annual reports typically run 100 to 300-plus pages; a single NVIDIA annual report (10-K) is roughly 340,000 characters. That is slow, every advisor does it a little differently, and the warning signs compliance cares most about, such as auditor problems, restatements or heavy insider selling, are easy to miss when skimming.

The goal: an advisor types a normal question, such as "Get me up to speed on Coca-Cola," about any of the roughly 10,400 companies on the SEC's official list, and within seconds gets three things: the current price and its six-month change, the key financials, and a plain-language summary of the latest SEC filings. Every answer links to its sources, and the tool never gives buy or sell recommendations.

Advisor Research Desk

3

Top band: the assistant reads the question, works out which company it is about and which workflow or workflows are needed, runs them, and returns one answer with sources. It keeps short-term memory of the conversation, so a follow-up like "any insider selling?" stays on the same company. Workflow 1, stock quote: match the company name to its official ticker, fetch six months of daily prices, and calculate the current price, six-month change and high/low with ordinary arithmetic. The output is the price plus a one-paragraph summary. The AI never produces these numbers. Workflow 2, SEC filings: find the company in the SEC's official list, then read four report types at the same time: the annual report (10-K, or 20-F for foreign companies), the quarterly report (10-Q), current-event reports (8-K or 6-K: earnings releases, deals, executive changes) and insider trades (Form 4). We keep management's discussion of results, top company-specific risks, outlook, buybacks, legal matters and insider buying and selling, and skip boilerplate. Red flags (control weaknesses, restatements, going-concern doubts, auditor changes) are checked by fixed rules, not AI. Key financials come from the structured financial data the SEC publishes. The output is one combined summary.

These are real responses from the two workflows for NVIDIA, fetched live on 2026-09-23. Workflow 1 returns two fields, a stock price and a summary: $225.28 as of 2026-09-23, up $49.64 (28.26 percent) over six months from $175.64 on 2026-03-23, with a six-month range of $165.17 to $235.74. Workflow 2, for the quarter ended 2026-07-26, from official SEC data: revenue $96.2 billion, up 106 percent from a year ago; gross profit $72.1 billion, up 113 percent; operating income $63.7 billion, up 124 percent; net income $59.7 billion, up 126 percent; diluted earnings per share $2.46, up 128 percent. Filings were read in about four seconds. Annual report (10-K, filed 2026-02-25): sections used were management's discussion and analysis, risk factors, business, buybacks and other information; no red flags found. Quarterly report (10-Q, filed 2026-08-26). Three recent event reports (8-K): earnings release on 2026-08-26, a material agreement plus new debt on 2026-08-17, and another event on 2026-09-03. Insider trades (Form 4) from September 2 to 22, 2026: 19 open-market sales totalling about $954 million and no purchases; 3 of the sales were made under pre-set 10b5-1 trading plans, which executives arrange in advance. The same works for any of about 10,400 SEC-registered companies, including foreign filers such as Toyota and TSMC, shown in their own currency.
